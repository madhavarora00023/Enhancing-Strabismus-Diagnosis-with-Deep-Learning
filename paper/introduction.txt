Introduction 

Strabismus, commonly referred to as ocular misalignment, is a prevalent visual disorder affecting both children and adults. It can lead to binocular vision disorders, depth perception deficiencies, and, in severe cases, amblyopia. Patients with strabismus often experience challenges in their daily lives, such as difficulty in reading, driving, and participating in sports due to impaired visual coordination. Additionally, social and psychological impacts, including reduced self-confidence and difficulties in maintaining eye contact, can significantly affect quality of life. Early and precise diagnosis is crucial for timely intervention, as untreated strabismus can result in long-term visual impairments and psychosocial challenges.

Traditional diagnostic methods rely on clinical assessments such as the Hirschberg test, cover tests, and prism evaluations, which are subjective and require specialized expertise. However, these methods lack automation and quantitative precision, limiting their scalability in resource-limited settings.

With advancements in artificial intelligence (AI) and deep learning, computer vision-based diagnostic tools have demonstrated significant potential in ophthalmology. Existing AI-powered models for strabismus detection primarily focus on classification tasks, distinguishing between normal and misaligned eye positions. While these methods have shown promising results, they often lack quantitative measurement capabilities, which are essential for clinical decision-making and treatment planning. There is a critical need for an integrated system that classifies strabismus subtypes and provides highly accurate predictions.

This study proposes a deep learning-based framework for comprehensive strabismus diagnosis, leveraging multiple state-of-the-art models to achieve high accuracy. Among these, EfficientNetB7 is the most accurate classification model, significantly improving diagnostic performance. In addition to EfficientNetB7, we incorporate ResNet50, VGG19, and AlexNet to compare and enhance classification accuracy. The framework identifies strabismus into five categories—esotropia, exotropia, hypertropia, hypotropia, and normal alignment—using open-source datasets.

By integrating multiple classification models, our framework aims to provide ophthalmologists with a robust, automated, and quantitative tool for diagnosing strabismus. This study represents a significant advancement in AI-driven ophthalmic diagnostics, facilitating early detection and personalized treatment planning.
